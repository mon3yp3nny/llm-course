1. What happens when you write something to a language model? Let us follow one short sentence through the machine.

  2. It starts with text: your prompt. The model never sees your intent, only these characters.

  3. First the text is cut into tokens. These are common pieces of text: often a whole word, sometimes only part of one.

  4. Each token is replaced by its number in the vocabulary. From here on, your prompt is just a sequence of numbers.

  5. Each number stands for a list of numbers, a vector. It places the token as a point in a space where similar words lie close together.

  6. The vectors travel through the neural network, a long stack of layers. Every layer rewrites them a little.

  7. On the way, each token looks back at the tokens before it and at itself, and takes what it needs. This is attention.

  8. At the end, every token in the vocabulary gets a probability: how likely is it to come next? What is scored is what is likely, not what is true.

  9. Then comes a lottery. A token with 45% holds 45 of 100 tickets. Usually a likely token wins, sometimes a surprising one.

  10. The token that was drawn is added to the text, and everything runs again. This is how the answer is written: one token at a time.

  11. At its core, that is all there is: text becomes numbers, the numbers pass through the network, one token is drawn. And then it starts again.
